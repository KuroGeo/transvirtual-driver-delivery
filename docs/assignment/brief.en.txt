Take-Home Assignment — Driver Delivery App
Mobile Engineer (Flutter) · TransVirtual · Candidate brief

Overview
Build a small, working slice of a driver's delivery app: a Flutter app for viewing today's delivery stops
and updating their status — designed offline-first, because our drivers work in depots and dead
zones. We care most that it runs end-to-end as a workable demo on an emulator or simulator, and
that your offline design survives a restart.

What we provide
   • deliveries.csv — 30 delivery stops with columns: reference, customer_name, address, latitude,
      longitude, status, time_window.
That is all. Local storage choice, sync design, state management, and every other tooling decision
are yours — deliberately. They are part of what we evaluate.

Requirements
   1. Data: bundle the provided deliveries.csv as an asset and load it into local storage of your
      choice (sqflite, Drift, Hive, Isar — your call).
   2. Stop list screen: today's stops with reference, customer, address, and status, filterable by
      status.
   3. Stop detail and status update: mark a stop completed, or failed with a required failure reason.
      Lifecycle: pending → completed | failed; completed and failed are terminal — invalid updates
      must be rejected in logic, not just hidden in the UI.
   4. Offline-first: every update persists locally and survives an app restart. Maintain a visible
      pending-sync queue in the UI.
   5. Sync: include an in-app connectivity toggle. While “offline”, updates accumulate in the queue;
      when switched “online”, the queue flushes to a mock sync target (a stub server, a logged
      endpoint, or an in-app fake — your design) and the UI reflects synced state.
   6. State management: your choice — be ready to defend it.
   7. Tests: at least two meaningful tests — we suggest one on the status lifecycle and one on the
      offline queue behaviour.
   8. Workable demo: the app runs on an Android emulator or iOS simulator with documented
      commands (flutter run is fine). Demo flow we'll follow: go offline → update several stops →
      restart the app → go online → watch the queue sync.

Ground rules
   • Timebox: 4–6 hours of effort, due within 2 days of receiving this brief (a 3rd day is available on
     request). Stop at the timebox and write down what you would do next — unfinished-but-well-
     reasoned beats gold-plated.
   • AI tools (Claude Code, Copilot, Cursor, etc.) are allowed and encouraged — we work AI-first.
     Include a short AI-usage note: which tools, what was generated vs. hand-written, and at least
     one thing the AI got wrong and how you caught it.
   • Use any packages you like. Skip authentication, real backend integration, push notifications,
     and anything not listed in the requirements.
   • You will demo this live (about 5 minutes) at the start of your final interview and extend it during
     the session — build something you can talk about.

What to submit
   • A Git repository link (or zip) with the full source.
   • README.md: how to run the demo (the exact commands and target emulator/simulator), key
     decisions — storage, sync, and state-management choices and why — and your “what I'd do
     next” list.
   • Your AI-usage note (in the README or separate).

How we evaluate
  • The demo is the deliverable: if we cannot run your app with the documented commands, we
    will ask you once to fix the setup; if it still doesn't run, we can't progress the application. The
    demo matters more than any individual feature.
  • We look at: offline and sync design · correctness of the status lifecycle · code structure and
    state management · setup and tooling choices · testing · the quality of decisions explained in
    your README · judgment in how you used AI tools.
  • In the final interview you'll demo the app, defend your design as we scale the constraints
    (thousands of stops, photo/signature payloads, conflict resolution, battery), and discuss how
    you'd help junior engineers pick up Flutter.
